## What I want to spend years on

**Hadith and Usul al-Hadith, Takhrij, Tahqiq and Shafi‘i fiqh** are where I want most of my serious academic work to go over the long term.

Usul al-Hadith is especially close to me because it forces you to slow down. A report is rarely just a line of text: there are routes, narrators, differences in wording, hidden defects, corroboration, disagreement between critics and a lot of judgement behind a final ruling.

And on the fiqh side, I study **Shafi‘i fiqh** and want to keep going deeper into the madhhab, its texts, its usul and the way its jurists reasoned through evidence.

I want the years ahead to include serious reading, research, writing, editing and publication in these fields.

## Atlas Reader

Atlas Reader came out of the same life.

I work with a lot of PDFs, and most readers never quite fit the way I wanted to study. I wanted to find books quickly, move through large files without friction, keep detailed bookmarks, work properly with Arabic and RTL material, annotate freely, and keep all of that work on my own machine.

So I started building the reader I wanted to use myself.

The current direction is **Windows-first with C++23 and Qt**, while keeping the core portable enough for other platforms later.

## Design, software, and study

I don’t really see these as separate boxes anymore. The more I study, the more ideas I get for tools, interfaces, publications and systems I wish existed. And the more I design or build, the more I notice problems in how Arabic, Urdu, books, references and long-form reading are handled digitally.

That overlap is where a lot of my work is going.
